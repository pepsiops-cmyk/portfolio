Self-initiated project · Lifting tracker app

A lifting log where a repeat set is one tap, and progress shows right away

My bet: people use a gym tracker to see progress, and logging is the price of that answer. So I designed the logging to take the fewest actions possible. Every set starts filled in from last time. A repeat set is one tap. A set one step better is two, where many trackers make you tap the number and type it again.

Problem

Result

A working prototype of logging, editing and review. Not tested with users yet. Next is a test with people who quit a tracker.

01 · Context

Logging is the cost. Progress is the product.

This is my own project. I lift, and I wanted to test one idea: people stop using gym trackers because logging is annoying, not because the tracker lacks features.

So the scope is narrow on purpose. No social feed, no program builder, no charts. Only the two moments that happen every workout: logging a set, and seeing how today went.

02 · Problem

Between sets, extra taps make logging feel like a chore

I started from what the lifter is trying to do between sets, and what they already expect.

1. They expect today to look like last time

Most sets are a repeat of last time, or a small step up: one more rep, or five more pounds. Many trackers already fill in last time. But a small step up still means tapping the number, typing a new one and confirming it.

Tap and type is shown as a common pattern. It is not a count from a specific app.

2. They hesitate on two questions

"What did I do last time?" and "Which plates make 180?" Both make the lifter stop, think, or leave the screen to find the answer. The design puts both answers where the lifter already looks.

03 · The calls

Make the common case one tap, and keep the rest close

Call 1

Every set starts as last time

Today's weight and reps are filled in from the last workout, and last time's numbers sit in their own column beside them. If the lifter did the same, they tap the check. If they did one step more, they tap + first. No typing.

Why
It matches what the lifter already expects. The app does the remembering, so the lifter doesn't have to.
Tradeoff
A quick tap can log last time's numbers by mistake. To keep that cheap to fix, editing the weight of a logged set un-checks it, so the lifter confirms it again.

Call 2

Big changes open a keypad that shows the plates

For a weight that isn't one step away, the lifter taps the number to open a keypad. It shows the change against last time, the plates to load on each side, quick steps, and the weights they often use.

Why
It answers "which plates make 180?" on the same screen, so the lifter doesn't do the math in their head or check another app.
Tradeoff
The keypad holds a lot. Worth it because it only opens when the lifter needs it. The common case never sees it.

Call 3

Show progress the moment it happens

When the last set of an exercise is logged, its card shows what went up: top weight, estimated max or total volume. At the end, the review sorts the workout into went up, same and went down, and marks the set that dropped.

Why
"Am I getting stronger?" is the reason to log at all. Answering it right away gives the lifter a reason to log the next set too.
Tradeoff
The review compares with last time only, not with months of history. That keeps it a one-glance answer. Long-term charts can come later.

Changing the plan without leaving the workout

Gyms are busy, and plans change. The prototype handles that on the same screen:

  • Swipe a set to remove it, with Undo.
  • Hold and drag an exercise to reorder it.
  • Replace an exercise from ones you've done before, each shown with when you last did it and your last weights.
04 · What I'd do next

Test it with people who quit a tracker

The prototype works end to end, but nobody outside of me has used it yet. The idea is only proven when someone else logs a workout with it.

  • Who: five lifters who stopped using a tracker. They know exactly what annoyed them.
  • What to measure: taps and seconds per set, in this design and in the tracker they used.
  • What to watch: where they hesitate, and whether the review answers "did I improve?" without help.
  • It works if: 4 of 5 log a full workout without help, with fewer taps per set than their old tracker.
